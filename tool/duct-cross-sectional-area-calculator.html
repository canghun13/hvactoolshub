How to use

  1. Choose the dimension unit and enter the actual clear inside diameter or width and height.
  2. Read the same cross-sectional area in ft², in², m², and mm² without converting the dimensions by hand.
  3. Use the area with an identified airflow in the Duct Velocity Calculator when face velocity is the next question.

Formulas

Round area: A = πD² ÷ 4
Rectangular area: A = W × H
Velocity from known airflow: V = Q ÷ A

Keep every dimension in the same length unit before applying a formula. The calculator converts the resulting area with exact international inch and foot relationships.

Worked examples

A 12 in round duct has 113.10 in², or 0.7854 ft², of cross-sectional area. A 100 mm round duct has 7,853.98 mm², or 0.007854 m². An 18 in × 8 in rectangular duct has 144 in², or 1.00 ft².

Result interpretation

Cross-sectional area is the clear geometric opening normal to the airflow. It is not exterior sheet-metal surface area, grille free area, or an equivalent-round diameter. Equal face area also does not mean equal friction loss: shape, wetted perimeter, roughness, fittings, length, and air conditions still matter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cross-sectional area of a 100 mm round duct?

Using A = πD² ÷ 4 with D = 100 mm gives 7,853.98 mm², which is 0.007854 m² before rounding.

Can I use outside duct dimensions?

Use clear inside dimensions for airflow area whenever available. Liner, insulation inside the shell, dampers, or other obstructions can reduce the usable opening.

Does equal area mean equal friction loss?

No. Equal area is only a geometry comparison. Round and rectangular ducts with the same area can have different wetted perimeter and resistance.

Is this the square footage of sheet metal?

No. This calculator finds the cross-sectional opening. Sheet-metal surface area also requires duct length and perimeter and answers a different question.
